The International Institute of Higher Education (IIHE) Cambodia offers two pathways to a UK Bachelor’s degree: **BSc (Hons) Business Management** and **BEng (Hons) Software Engineering**. Both follow the same four-stage structure and the same fees. The difference is what you study and the kind of work it prepares you for.

## At a glance

| | Business Management | Software Engineering |
|---|---|---|
| Final award | BSc (Hons) Business Management | BEng (Hons) Software Engineering |
| Core focus | How organisations grow, compete and lead | How software and digital systems are designed and built |
| You will learn | Management, marketing, finance, people, operations, strategy | Programming, web development, databases, networks, testing |
| How you learn | Classes, case work, assignments and assessments | Computer labs, practical projects and assessments |
| Coding needed to start? | No | No, you can begin at Foundation level |

## Business Management may suit you if…

- you enjoy understanding why some businesses succeed and others do not
- you are interested in marketing, finance, people or running your own business one day
- you like discussion, presentations and working with others on real problems

The programme builds skills relevant to marketing, banking and finance, human resources, operations, trade, business development, management and entrepreneurship.

## Software Engineering may suit you if…

- you like solving problems step by step and seeing something you built actually work
- you are curious about how apps, websites and business systems are made
- you are ready to practise regularly; programming is learned by doing

The programme builds skills relevant to software development, web and mobile applications, databases, software testing, systems analysis, business analysis and technology consulting.

## Three questions to ask yourself

1. **Which subject would I still enjoy in year three?** Interest keeps you going when the work gets harder.
2. **Do I prefer people problems or technical problems?** Both programmes involve both, but the balance is different.
3. **What kind of work do I picture myself doing?** Look at the career directions above and see which list excites you more.

Career outcomes always depend on your experience, performance and local employment requirements.
